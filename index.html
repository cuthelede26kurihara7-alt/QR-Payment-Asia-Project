Beyond Cards:
How Southeast Asia Built a New Digital Payment Infrastructure

Southeast Asia is building digital payments without following the traditional card-first model.

For decades, digital payments have been closely associated with bank accounts, payment cards, and traditional financial networks.

However, mobile technology has created a different pathway. Across Southeast Asia, consumers and businesses are adopting digital payments through mobile-enabled ecosystems rather than following the card-first model used in many developed economies.

Digital Payments Are Growing Globally — But Not Everywhere in the Same Way

Digital merchant payments are expanding worldwide, but adoption varies significantly across economies. Some markets have developed mature digital payment ecosystems, while others are still building the infrastructure needed for wider participation.

Southeast Asia Does Not Have One Digital Payment Story

Countries are reaching digital payments through different combinations of financial access, mobile connectivity, and merchant networks.

Thailand and Malaysia show strong adoption of digital merchant payments alongside high levels of financial access, while Indonesia and the Philippines continue expanding digital participation through mobile-based infrastructure.

Low Cards, High Digital Payments

The most surprising pattern appears when comparing card payment usage with digital merchant payment adoption.

Malaysia and Thailand achieved high levels of digital merchant payment adoption despite very low card payment usage. This suggests that digital payment ecosystems do not always need to develop through a traditional card-first pathway.

Beyond Cards

Southeast Asia did not skip digital payments. It built a different path.

By combining mobile connectivity, financial inclusion, and digital infrastructure, countries can create new ways for consumers and businesses to participate in the digital economy.

The future of payments will not follow one universal blueprint. Different economies are building different bridges into the digital economy.
